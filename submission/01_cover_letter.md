## Why GigaByte

This work presents a fully reproducible, end-to-end computational pipeline
that re-analyzes three publicly available 16S rRNA metagenomic cohorts
(Nielsen et al., 2014, n=318; Ijaz et al., 2017, n=50; HMP2/IBDMDB,
n=130) to establish whether the taxonomic dysbiosis signature of
inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is reproducible and transportable across
independent cohorts. The accompanying GitHub repository
(https://github.com/ejtettevi/ibd-taxonomic-dysbiosis) contains the complete
analysis code, conda environment definitions, and configuration needed to
regenerate every figure, table, and scorecard from the raw data via a single
command (`run_all.sh`). We believe this is precisely the type of sound,
reproducible research-data analysis that GigaByte was created to publish:
a computational method (ANCOM-BC2 + random-effects meta-analysis + nested
leave-one-cohort-out random-forest classification) that has been implemented
in code, tested across three independent cohorts, and released openly with
full provenance.

Given GigaByte's focus on reproducibility-supported research objects rather
than novelty-driven claims, and its 100% APC waiver for researchers based in
Ghana (the corresponding author's country), GigaByte is the ideal venue for
this manuscript.
